How it's built

Four triangles, one glider.

Each plane is four flat triangles (two wings, two keel folds) drawn with a hand-rolled 3D projection on a normal 2D canvas, with no 3D library. They don't follow a scripted path: each one has a velocity and simple glider rules, and its body is turned to point wherever it's going. That's why a knocked plane tumbles, loses height, and then flies again on its own.

There's no recording for this one yet. It's built from the idea of tol.is's “Paper plane” and from your brief: planes in space, touch one and it reacts, then it goes back to flying. When you send a recording I'll match the original's motion and sound more closely.

Ingredients

  • Perspective projectionscreenX = W/2 + x / z × f. Dividing by depth is all that makes far things small.
  • Rotation matricesYaw, pitch and roll turn the four triangles' corners from “plane space” into the world.
  • Painter's algorithmSort every triangle by depth and draw back to front, so near wings cover far ones.
  • Flat shadingBrightness = how directly each triangle faces the light (a dot product of two directions).
  • Steering + glider rulesCruise in one direction, swoop on a slow sine, drift side to side, keep inside the view.
  • BankingRoll in proportion to how fast the plane is turning, like a real one.
  • Tumble stateA 1→0 value that hands control from physics (gravity, spin) back to flight.
  • Screen-space hit testProject each plane's centre and pick the closest one under your finger.
  • Stereo-panned Web AudioWhooshes and paper crinkles come from the plane's side of the screen; the wind is a looped, filtered noise.
  • Depth cuesFog, drifting dust and slow clouds make the space read as deep.

Building a plane out of four triangles

Corners are written in the plane's own space: x forward, y up, z to the right. The wings tilt up slightly at the tips (dihedral, which is what keeps real gliders stable) and the keel folds hang underneath.

const NOSE = [1.0, 0, 0];
const L_TIP = [-0.7, 0.08, -0.62], R_TIP = [-0.7, 0.08, 0.62];
const L_ROOT = [-0.7, 0, -0.04],  R_ROOT = [-0.7, 0, 0.04];
const KEEL = [-0.7, -0.22, 0];

const TRIS = [
  [NOSE, L_TIP, L_ROOT],   // left wing
  [NOSE, R_ROOT, R_TIP],   // right wing
  [NOSE, L_ROOT, KEEL],    // left keel fold
  [NOSE, KEEL, R_ROOT],    // right keel fold
];

Rotate into the world, then project to the screen

Each corner is rolled, then pitched, then turned to the plane's heading, scaled, and moved to its position. Then perspective: divide by depth. That's the whole 3D engine.

// roll about x, pitch about z, yaw about y
y1 = y*cos(roll) - z*sin(roll);   z1 = y*sin(roll) + z*cos(roll);
x2 = x*cos(pitch) - y1*sin(pitch); y2 = x*sin(pitch) + y1*cos(pitch);
x3 = x2*cos(yaw) - z1*sin(yaw);   z3 = x2*sin(yaw) + z1*cos(yaw);

sx = W/2 + (plane.x + x3*size) / (plane.z + z3*size) * f;
sy = H/2 - (plane.y + y2*size) / (plane.z + z3*size) * f;

Fly where you're going

Planes are steered by velocity, not by angles. Each frame the velocity eases toward a “desired” one: the cruise direction, plus a slow sine for swooping, a drift for wandering, and soft springs that keep it in view. The body angles are then read off the velocity: heading from the horizontal direction, nose-up/down from the climb, and a bank proportional to how fast the heading is changing.

yawTarget   = Math.atan2(v.z, v.x);
pitchTarget = Math.atan2(v.y, Math.hypot(v.x, v.z));
rollTarget  = turnRate * config.bank;     // lean into the turn

Knock, tumble, recover

A tap sends a push away from your finger plus some lift, and a spin on roll and pitch. It also sets tumble = 1. While tumbling, gravity and drag act on the plane, the spin carries on and slowly dies down, and steering is turned down. tumble falls back to 0 over Time to recover, so the plane gradually takes back control, levels out and rejoins the others.

const ctrl = 1 - p.tumble;              // 0 = falling paper, 1 = flying
p.vy -= GRAVITY * p.tumble * dt;
steer(p, desired, rate * ctrl);
p.roll += p.spinRoll * dt;   p.spinRoll *= Math.exp(-2.5 * dt);
p.roll += wrap(rollTarget - p.roll) * (1 - Math.exp(-6 * ctrl * dt));
p.tumble = Math.max(0, p.tumble - dt / config.recover);

Three ways to look at the flight

The camera never moves; only the planes' cruise direction changes. In the side view it sits a little above the flock and tilts down about 10°, so you see the tops of the wings instead of thin edges. That's one more rotation applied to every point before the perspective divide. Side flies them right → left across the view, Behind sends them away from you (you see their tails shrink into the fog), and Toward brings them out of the distance at you. When a plane leaves the view it's quietly recycled at the opposite edge.

Sound that comes from the plane

Every effect is panned by the plane's horizontal position on screen, so a plane hit on the right sounds right. The paper crinkle is 6–10 tiny noise bursts at random high pitches within 150ms; the whoosh is noise through a filter sweeping upward; the wind is a looped noise through a low-pass filter whose cutoff and volume drift slowly.

// crinkle
for (let i = 0; i < 8; i++) synth.noise({
  filter: "bandpass", freq: rand(2000, 6500), q: 3,
  dur: rand(0.015, 0.035), delay: rand(0, 0.15), pan,
});
// whoosh
synth.noise({ filter: "bandpass", freq: 300, to: 1600, q: 1.2, dur: 0.4, attack: 0.08, pan });
