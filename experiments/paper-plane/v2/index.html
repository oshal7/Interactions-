How it's built · v2

Nothing to do, something to feel.

v1 was a toy: tap to knock, drag to throw. v2 takes the control away. Planes live in the sky around you and drift on slow air currents in every direction. Your finger doesn't hit them or make new ones; it moves the air, and planes caught in that air do what you did. Hold still and they slowly circle you.

Ingredients

  • 3D flow fieldA wind made of layered sine waves through space and time, giving smooth, swirling currents everywhere.
  • Boids (light)Separation, alignment, cohesion: the three rules that make birds (and planes) move as loose flocks.
  • Soft boundaryThe further outside an invisible bubble a plane drifts, the harder it turns back. No edges, no respawning.
  • A gesture gridYour movement is written into a coarse grid over the screen that slowly fades, like a wake in water.
  • Screen → world velocityPixels per second at your finger become world units per second at each plane's depth, so they move exactly like you on screen.
  • Orbit steeringHolding still turns the pull into a sideways (tangent) push around your fingertip.
  • Plane renderer from v1Same four triangles, rotation, projection and depth sort.
  • Generative musicA chord from a pentatonic scale every few seconds, slow attack and long release, through a synthetic reverb.

The air: a flow field you can't see

Every point in space has a wind direction made from a few overlapping sine waves of position and time. Nearby points have similar wind, so planes close together drift together, and the pattern slowly changes over time, so the sky never repeats.

function flow(x, y, z, t) {
  return [
    Math.sin(y * 0.35 + t * 0.07) + Math.sin(z * 0.21 - t * 0.05),
    Math.sin(z * 0.28 + t * 0.06) * 0.5 + Math.sin(x * 0.19 + t * 0.04) * 0.4,
    Math.sin(x * 0.31 - t * 0.06) + Math.sin(y * 0.23 + t * 0.08),
  ];
}

Loose flocks

Each plane looks at its neighbours: it moves away from any that are too close (separation), turns a little toward their average heading (alignment), and drifts a little toward their centre (cohesion). Flock together scales the last two. A light touch gives loose, breathing groups instead of a tight swarm.

Your finger moves the air, not the planes

The screen is divided into a coarse grid. As you move, your velocity (px/s) is added to the cells around your finger, strongest at the centre. Every frame the whole grid fades a little (How long the air remembers). Each plane reads the grid where it appears on screen and converts that to world speed at its own depth, so near and far planes move the same distance on screen as your finger did. That's why they seem to copy you.

// write: add finger velocity to nearby cells
cell.vx += pointer.vx * falloff * dt * 4;

// read: screen px/s → world units/s at the plane's depth
const want = [g.vx * z / focal, -g.vy * z / focal, 0];
const w = clamp(Math.hypot(...want) / pace, 0, 1) * config.follow;
desired = mix(natural, want, w);

Hold still and they circle you

When your finger is down but barely moving, nearby planes steer toward a ring around it: a sideways push (perpendicular to the line between plane and finger) keeps them going round, and a gentle pull keeps the ring from drifting out. Lift your finger and the flow field slowly takes them back.

Generative, calm sound

Every 5–8 seconds a three-note chord is picked from a pentatonic scale (no note ever clashes) and played with a 2.5-second fade-in and a long tail through a synthetic reverb (a convolver fed decaying noise). When planes start following your gesture, a soft high note from the same scale chimes, panned to where you are, at most every few hundred ms. Under it, a quiet wind.
