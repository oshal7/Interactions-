How it's built · v1

Pendulums, and the sound of metal.

A wind chime is a handful of pendulums that keep bumping into each other. Everything here is simulated: the tubes swing under gravity, hit each other and the striker with real impulses, and every hit is synthesised from the physics of a vibrating tube. How hard, where and what hit what all change the sound. The 3D is drawn with three.js (WebGL); the physics and the audio are our own code.

No recording for this one. It's built from your brief and from how real tuned chimes work. The default tuning is a calm pentatonic set, like the ones well-known tuned chimes (Nature's Melody, Corinthian Bells…) are built around.

The anatomy

PartIn a real chimeIn the simulation
Top discHolds everything; hangs from a hook.Fixed. Each tube's string is pinned to a point on its rim.
TubesAluminium, length sets the note. Each hangs on a short cord.A pendulum (string + tube in one line) with mass ∝ length. Length from the note: L ∝ 1/√f.
StrikerA wooden puck on the centre cord, at the tubes' sweet spot.A pendulum; collides as a horizontal disc at its own height.
SailA paddle below the striker that catches the wind.Wind force on the sail acts on the striker through the lever of its longer string.
Your handBrushing the tubes.A thin capsule along the view ray under your cursor, moving with it; pushes but never rings.

Ingredients

  • three.js (WebGL)Metal tubes lit by an environment map, soft shadows on a back wall, thin line strings.
  • Verlet integrationStore the position and the previous one; velocity is the difference. Constraints become simple projections.
  • Distance constraintsAfter each step, put the particle back at its string length from the pivot. That's a pendulum.
  • Substeps10 physics steps per frame, so fast swings don't tunnel through each other.
  • Segment–segment distanceTubes are capsules; the closest points between two line segments give contact, normal and depth.
  • Impulses with lever armsA hit near the bottom of a tube moves it more than one near the top, as with a real pendulum.
  • Restitution + air dragHow bouncy hits are, and how slowly swinging fades. Low drag = long, smooth settling.
  • Free-free bar acousticsOvertones at 1, 2.756, 5.404, 8.933× the fundamental: the bell-like, un-musical partials of a hanging tube.
  • Velocity → loudness and brightnessHarder hits are louder and richer; brushes are soft and pure.
  • Convolution reverb + panningEach hit comes from where the tube is, in a soft room.

A tube is a pendulum

Each body is one particle (its centre of mass) and a fixed pivot. Verlet integration moves it by its own momentum plus gravity; then a constraint snaps it back to the right distance from the pivot. That's all it takes to swing in 3D, in any direction, including circles.

const v = sub(b.X, b.Xp);                    // implied velocity
b.Xp = b.X;
b.X = add(b.X, add(mul(v, drag), mul(gravity, dt * dt)));
b.X = add(b.P, mul(norm(sub(b.X, b.P)), b.Lc));   // stay on the string

Finding where two tubes touch

A tube is a line segment with a radius (a capsule). The closest points between two segments tell us if they overlap (distance < r₁ + r₂), where (the contact point), and which way to push (the normal). The striker is a flat puck, so for it we compare positions at the puck's height.

const c = closestSegSeg(a0, a1, b0, b1);   // Ericson, Real-Time Collision Detection
const dist = len(sub(c.pa, c.pb));
if (dist < A.r + B.r) resolve(A, B, normal, depth);

Bouncing apart, the right amount

Each body rotates around its pivot, so a point at height h moves h/L as fast as the centre. That ratio also sets how “heavy” the tube feels at the contact point. With it we compute one impulse that sends both tubes apart with the chosen bounciness. Because velocity in Verlet is X − Xp, applying the impulse just means shifting Xp.

const rA = hA / A.Lc, wA = rA * rA / A.m;      // inverse mass at the contact
const vn = dot(sub(vA, vB), n);                // closing speed
if (vn < 0) {
  const j = -(1 + bounce) * vn / (wA + wB);
  A.Xp = sub(A.Xp, mul(n, j * rA / A.m * dt));  // Δv = j·r/m
  B.Xp = add(B.Xp, mul(n, j * rB / B.m * dt));
  ring(A, -vn); ring(B, -vn);                    // the sound gets the same speed
}

Coming back slowly

Nothing pulls the tubes back to rest except gravity and a little air drag (air ≈ 0.12/s). So after a hit they swing out, come back, maybe touch again, and settle over many seconds, the way real chimes do. Raise Air resistance to calm them faster, lower Bounce for duller, heavier collisions.

The sound of a hanging tube

A tube that's free at both ends (a “free-free bar”) doesn't vibrate like a string. Its overtones sit at 2.756×, 5.404× and 8.933× the fundamental. Those inharmonic partials are the shimmering, bell-like quality of chimes. Each hit starts four sine partials with an instant attack; higher ones fade much faster. A tiny bandpassed noise burst adds the metallic “tick” (duller when the wooden striker hits). A second, 0.1%-detuned fundamental creates slow beating, and a gentle tremolo stands in for the tube turning on its cord. Lower notes ring longer.

const MODES = [1, 2.756, 5.404, 8.933];
const DECAY = [1, 0.42, 0.18, 0.08];          // × ring time (≈ 7.5 s at A4)
amp = min(1, speed / 0.9) ** 1.15 * 0.22;     // harder hit = louder
brightness grows with speed and hardness;      // and richer

Tuning

Tube length follows the note: for a free-free bar, frequency ∝ 1/L², so the length is ∝ 1/√f. The presets are calm scales with no clashing notes: a nature-style major pentatonic, the Japanese In scale, a minor pentatonic, an open major-9th “healing” chord and a deep tenor set. Pitch transposes all of them.

Other ways to build it

  • Rapier / cannon-esFull rigid-body engines with hinge joints and capsules. More general, heavier to load.
  • Recorded samplesRecord each tube once and play with velocity-based volume. Realistic, but every tuning needs new recordings.
  • Modal synthesis with resonant filtersExcite a bank of narrow bandpass filters with a click; very efficient for many voices.
