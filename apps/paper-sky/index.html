Paper Sky

The drifting paper-plane sky from Paper plane v2, on your Mac and your phone. On the Mac: as the screen saver when you lock up, as a live wallpaper that follows your cursor, and as a full-screen ambient mode you can play with, with sound.

This is the exact page the Mac app runs. Open it full screen in a tab ↗.

Three ways to have it.

Screen saverPlanes drift on their own, with a slow invisible “hand” that sometimes draws loops through them, and a clock. Shows whenever your Mac locks or idles.Watch-only: macOS ends screen savers on any input · 30 fps · no sound
Live wallpaperBehind your desktop icons. The planes follow your cursor as you work. Stops drawing when windows cover it.Menu-bar app · 30 fps · sound optional
Ambient modeFull screen and fully interactive: move to guide them, hold still to make them circle you, with the ambient pad, chimes and wind. Esc to leave.Menu-bar app · 60 fps · sound on

Install

↓ Download for macOS Latest builds (GitHub Actions)

macOS 12 Monterey or newer · Apple Silicon and Intel · two small zips (screen saver + app).

  1. Screen saver: unzip PaperSky-ScreenSaver, double-click Paper Sky.saver, choose Install.
  2. App: unzip PaperSky-App, drag Paper Sky.app to Applications, open it. A paper plane appears in your menu bar.
  3. First launch: the builds are signed ad-hoc, not notarized by Apple, so macOS warns it can't verify the developer. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway (or right-click the file › Open).
  4. Use it as your lock screen: in System Settings › Screen Saver pick Paper Sky. In System Settings › Lock Screen set “Start Screen Saver when inactive” and “Require password after screen saver begins” to Immediately.
  5. Lock on demand: menu bar ✈︎ › Lock now starts the screen saver right away. Move the mouse or press a key, and macOS asks for your password. (macOS's own ⌃⌘Q locks too, but shows the standard lock screen until the screen saver kicks in.)
Why it isn't a custom password screen. macOS doesn't let apps replace the real lock or login screen, and an app that looks like one would be a security risk: it wouldn't actually lock your Mac (other apps keep running, ⌘-Tab still works), and it would be handling your login password. Paper Sky never sees your password. It's the picture; macOS is the lock. The trade-off is that the screen saver can't be interactive, because macOS dismisses screen savers on any mouse movement. For play, use Ambient mode.

On your phone

Phones decide what may run on the lock screen, so each platform gets the best it allows. Your PIN, pattern or Face ID always stays with the phone; Paper Sky never sees it.

Android · live wallpaper A real live wallpaper app. Home screen: fully interactive, planes follow your finger, and swiping between pages adds a gentle parallax. Lock screen: the sky keeps moving on its own (Android keeps lock-screen touches for itself, so an invisible hand draws slow loops through the planes). The app itself is a full-screen interactive sky with settings. Android 8.0+ · 30 fps (20 in Battery Saver) · stops when not visible
iPhone · home-screen app iOS doesn't allow animated or interactive lock screens from apps. Add Paper Sky to your Home Screen instead: it opens full screen, follows your finger, plays the ambient sound, shows an optional clock, and works offline. Safari › Share › Add to Home Screen
iPhone · lock-screen wallpaper The Wallpaper studio saves the sky at your iPhone's exact resolution as a still, or records a short loop you can turn into a Live Photo wallpaper (iOS plays it briefly when you wake the phone). Any iPhone · iOS 17+ for Live Photo wallpapers

  1. Android: download the APK on your phone and open it. Allow “Install unknown apps” for your browser when asked (it isn't from the Play Store). Open Paper Sky › ⋯ › Set as wallpaper › Home and lock screens.
  2. iPhone, interactive: open the iPhone app page in Safari › Share › Add to Home Screen. Tap the icon; tap once to start the sound. The ⋯ button switches mood, clock and sound.
  3. iPhone, lock screen: open the Wallpaper studio on the iPhone, pick your model, Save still › Save Image, then set it from Photos › Share › Use as Wallpaper. For motion: Record 6 s loop, convert it to a Live Photo with a video-to-Live-Photo app, and set that.

What it uses, and how it saves.

Measured in Chrome on a cloud Linux machine (per frame: physics for every plane + drawing); Apple Silicon is typically faster. Memory figures are estimates for macOS's web view, not measurements.

  • CPUThe planes' physics is tiny (a few dozen planes, ~0.1 ms). Most of each frame is drawing. The main lever is frame rate: wallpaper and screen saver run at 30 fps, low power at 20.
  • GPUOne full-screen 2D canvas, composited. Cost scales with pixels × fps, so we render at 1.5× instead of Retina's 2× (44% fewer pixels; 1× in low power is 75% fewer). Trails are off in the wallpaper.
  • Memory (estimate)One web-view process per screen, typically ~80–150 MB, plus ~20–30 MB for the app. The canvas itself is ~12 MB at 1.5×. The app never builds up garbage per frame.
  • SoundOff by default in the wallpaper and screen saver. When on: a 2.5 s reverb and a few oscillators, roughly 1–3% CPU (estimate). Paused with everything else.
  • BatteryStops drawing completely when the wallpaper is covered (full-screen apps, maximised windows), when the display sleeps, and while the Mac is locked. Switches to low power automatically in macOS Low Power Mode.
  • Network, diskNone at runtime, nothing is downloaded. About 1 MB on disk.

How it's put together

One web page, three hosts

apps/paper-sky/web/ is the v2 sky packaged as a single classic script (no ES modules, so it loads from disk inside a native web view) with options in the URL: ?mode=screensaver&fps=30&dpr=1.5&planes=28&mood=dusk&sound=0&clock=1. A frame cap skips vsyncs to hold the target fps, and window.paperSky lets the native side pause, resume, change settings and feed it the cursor.

The screen saver

A ScreenSaverView subclass (Swift) that adds a WKWebView and loads the bundled page in screen-saver mode. It listens for the system's “screen saver will stop” notification to pause immediately, since newer macOS versions don't always call stopAnimation().

The menu-bar app

An NSStatusItem app with no Dock icon. The live wallpaper is a borderless window at the desktop window level (above the picture, below the icons) that ignores the mouse, so the desktop still works. The app reads the global cursor 20–30 times a second and passes it to the page, which is how planes follow you while you work. Ambient mode is a key window above the menu bar; Esc closes it. “Lock now” launches the system ScreenSaverEngine, which is the real lock.

Built on GitHub

.github/workflows/paper-sky-macos.yml runs apps/paper-sky/macos/build.sh on a macOS runner: swiftc for Apple Silicon and Intel, lipo into universal binaries, bundle folders, ad-hoc codesign, zips. Every push uploads the zips as a build artifact; running the workflow by hand with “release” ticked publishes them as a GitHub Release. To build yourself on a Mac: bash apps/paper-sky/macos/build.sh.
